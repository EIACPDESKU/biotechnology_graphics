DNA Takes Control

The J. Craig Venter experiment demonstrated that synthetic DNA could take control of an existing bacterial cell and direct its growth and division.

Block 44: Synthetic Biology — J. Craig Venter Experiment

2010 Experiment — J. Craig Venter & Team

The experiment demonstrated that a cell could be controlled by a chemically synthesised copy of bacterial DNA.

1
Study the bacterial genome

The team studied the complete DNA sequence of the bacterium Mycoplasma mycoides.

2
Synthesise the DNA

An exact copy of the bacterial DNA was chemically synthesised in the laboratory.

3
Empty a related bacterial cell

DNA was removed from a related bacterium while its cytoplasm and cell membrane were kept intact.

4
Insert the synthetic DNA

The laboratory-made DNA was inserted into the emptied bacterial cell.

5
The cell follows the new instructions

The cell grew and divided, following the instructions encoded in the newly introduced synthetic DNA.

Key Conclusion

DNA controls the structure and activities of a cell.

Important: The cell was not created from scratch. Only its DNA was synthetic; the existing cell provided the cytoplasm and membrane needed for cellular activity.
