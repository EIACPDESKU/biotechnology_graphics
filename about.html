Green Skill Development Programme

GSDP under the Environmental Information, Awareness, Capacity Building and Livelihood Programme (EIACP), with a focused profile of the EIACP Centre on Environmental Biotechnology at the University of Kalyani.

PURPOSE

Green employment

Build technical environmental skills that can support gainful employment and self-employment, especially among youth and school dropouts.

ALIGNMENT

Sustainable development

GSDP was designed to contribute to India's environmental and development commitments, including NDCs, SDGs, biodiversity targets and waste-management goals.

DELIVERY

Distributed expertise

MoEFCC uses the ENVIS/EIACP network and specialist institutions to deliver practical courses across biodiversity, pollution monitoring, waste, water, forests and related fields.

Timeline

Selected milestones connecting national GSDP development with Kalyani University activity.

JUNE 2017

National GSDP pilot

MoEFCC launched pilot training for Biodiversity Conservationists and Para-taxonomists at 10 locations across nine biogeographic regions.

2018

Scale-up announced

MoEFCC announced more than 30 skilling programmes at 84 institutions, with a stated target of 80,000 people and later ambitions for much larger coverage.

2019

Kalyani: PBR training

The Kalyani EIACP centre was associated with GSDP training on People's Biodiversity Registers.

2023

National footprint

A published account reported 44 GSDP courses at 87 locations, with course durations broadly ranging from 80 to 550 hours.

SEPTEMBER 2026

Kalyani: soil pollution course

The University announced a 75-day Soil Pollution Monitoring Specialist GSDP course, tentatively beginning in the first week of September 2026.

What the evidence says about achievement

Nationally: the pilot produced 246 successful completions — 94 Biodiversity Conservationists and 152 Para-taxonomists. The programme subsequently expanded into a much wider course portfolio.

At Kalyani: the strongest public evidence demonstrates continuing institutional delivery through specialised courses. However, publicly accessible sources identified for this research do not provide a verified Kalyani-wide total of trainees, completion rate, placement rate, income change, or quantified livelihood outcomes.

Interpretation: continuity of specialised training is a positive institutional indicator, but it should not be presented as proof of employment impact without alumni and placement data.

Evidence & research limitations

A responsible institutional profile should separate documented facts from inference.

DOCUMENTED

National programme design

MoEFCC's public material documents the purpose, target groups, pilot results, course domains, NSQF compliance and national scale-up ambitions.

DOCUMENTED

Kalyani's programme notices

University material documents the DESKU EIACP centre and the 2026 Soil Pollution Monitoring Specialist course announcement.

NOT PUBLICLY VERIFIED

Outcome statistics

Course-wise enrolment, completion, placement, income, employer linkage, detailed expenditure and a complete staffing chart were not found in the evidence assembled for this page.

Recommendations for stronger impact reporting

1. Publish a course dashboard

Report seats, applicants, selected trainees, attendance, completion and certification by course and year.

2. Track alumni outcomes

Follow trainees for 6–24 months and record employment, self-employment, internships and environmental project participation.

3. Build employer linkages

Formalise links with pollution-control, biodiversity, laboratories, waste-management, environmental consulting and conservation organisations.
